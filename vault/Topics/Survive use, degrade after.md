# Survive use, degrade after

> How can materials survive use and climate extremes, yet biodegrade after disposal: reliable triggers or dedicated collection systems?

**Theme:** [[Theme 6 · Ocean & extreme environments]]  
**Scale:** macro · **Working group:** WG3 (proposed) · **Pillar:** climate · **Value:** sustainability

## Discussions behind this

- [[Why are most man-made materials dehydrated]] (RM003)

## Linked ideas

- *extends* → [[When to let materials die]]: If a material is designed to live, the choice to stop caring for it is the end-of-life trigger.
- *echoes* → [[Hydrated materials]]: 'Biology always wins' in a wet material: the same lack of agency over degradation that makes reliable biodegradation triggers so hard to design.
- *echoes* → [[Discovery vs reuse]]: Every new material creates a future end-of-life problem; designing reliable degradation or collection is the other half of discovery.
- *echoes* → [[Signalling & crosstalk]]: Robust enough to survive use, adaptable enough to give way afterwards: the same design tension at the scale of a product's whole life.
- *echoes* → [[Chronic bioelectrodes]]: Implanted conductive polymers can degrade on timescales similar to metal electrodes: designing something that lasts long enough in the body, then yields, mirrors the durable-then-degradable problem.
