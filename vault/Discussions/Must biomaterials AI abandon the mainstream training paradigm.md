# Must biomaterials AI abandon the mainstream training paradigm?

*RM009 (2026-08-20)* · notes RM009.4

## Provocation

> Modern ML solutions revolutionises protein design, however at their current stage they come with fundamental limitations stemming from their architecture and training data. The single percentage point inaccuracies can seem okay on benchmarks but are the distinction between working and failed drug in real life. At the same time there are no functional models that predict with high accuracy emergent properties in the cell. Given the documented failure of ML in a simpler data reach domain of protein folding, is it reasonable to believe that to build viable predictive and generative models for biomaterials we would need to abandon the current mainstream AI training paradigm (scaling laws, pre + post training) in favor of an approach that incorporates the constraints unique to biology?
