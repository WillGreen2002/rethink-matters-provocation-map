# Predicting unstable and disordered peptides — and the risks they carry

*Proposed*

## Provocation

> Current prediction models fail at predicting unstable conformations of proteins. How can we develop prediction models for intrinsically disordered peptide chains, most notoriously found in isoforms of prion protein (PrP) which causes significant neurodegenerative disease such as spongiform encephalopathy from accumulation of amyloid plaques. How can we develop computational models and empirical assays to identify this, and is it a major risk? If this disease is progressive over a 10 year period, how can that be modelled as well? Is there any other organ toxicity risk besides the brain? Will these de novo peptides be more stable if they have non-canonical aa in the sequence?
