# Bacterial cellulose ≠ wood

*Vancouver edition (2026-06-08)*

## Provocation

> Maybe the problem is that we keep imagining bacterial cellulose as a substitute for wood, when wood is already close to unbeatable: self-assembling, hierarchical, load-bearing, cheap, scalable, carbon-storing, and solar-powered.
> 
> Bacterial cellulose is impressive in the lab, but trees produce extraordinary structural materials. So perhaps the right question is not: How do we make bacterial cellulose replace wood?
> 
> The better question may be: What can bacterial cellulose do that wood never could?
> 
> Or to even ponder: What would a tree look like if we could have engineered it from day one?

## Discussion points

- What properties of bacterial cellulose are not “wood-like” and should be treated as advantages?
- How can we overcome the hydration problem in bacterial cellulose? 98–99% of its wet mass is water.
- What would a cellulose-based material system look like if it were designed for cities, medicine, electronics or climate adaptation from first principles?
- What forms of growth, patterning, responsiveness, repair, hybridity, porosity, sensing or local fabrication can we introduce to bacterial cellulose? How?

## Post-meeting synthesis (anonymised digest)

**Group discussions**
- Cellulose is needed generally and high-volume production is possible; purer compounds need more applied uses; until deeper research on complexation for more diverse applications, cellulose can be compared to wood simply as a source of cellulose.
- Forestry, life-cycle assessment and economics: views differed on how favourable sourcing from wood is compared with alternatives, and old-growth forest is very valuable and increasingly contested. Little will change until businesses are convinced that new solutions make money and are made to account for their waste; research tends to aim at impact rather than at being convenient and convincing for companies.
- Imagining a tree engineered from day one: no vertical growth? remove hemicellulose? focus on cellulose (simple structure, good mechanical properties)? a renewable source of aromatic carbon, but more linear? Planks with easy-peel bark (just for packaging), no leaves, no processing.
- What can bacterial cellulose do that wood never could? Ion transport for batteries; underwater bags or nets for divers.

**Combined session**
- Bacterial cellulose lacks the complexity of wood (about six levels of hierarchy: molecular, fibril, cell, tissue, rings, tree); wood by-products are not arbitrary.
- Demand for cellulose has not yet outstripped what agro-industry already provides.
- A claim that bacterial cellulose synthase uses less ATP than the plant enzyme was questioned: it depends on whether you count the whole pathway to UDP-glucose and UDP→UTP regeneration.
