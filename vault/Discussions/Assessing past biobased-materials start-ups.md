# Assessing past biobased-materials start-ups

*RM007 (2026-07-11)* · notes RM007.4

## Provocation

> Assessing past biobased materials startups (esp implosion circa 2023-2024). Primary example are in textile startups (several well-known ones). What tensions caused them to fail expectations? Timing, investor expectations, product-market fit, sustainability claims and regulation/policy hurdles. What's the latest on biobased material companies?

## Post-meeting synthesis (anonymised digest)

- **Why look back?** The “second wave” of companies is struggling with venture-capital funding, so how do you build a biomaterial company? Tensions behind earlier disappointments: timing, investor expectations, product–market fit, sustainability claims, regulation and policy hurdles.
- **Range of examples discussed:** stretchable bio-based formulations (a balloon replacement), mycelium-based materials, bacterial-cellulose products, a universal peptide adhesive inspired by mussel glue, elastic dampers. A pivot under delivery pressure was cited as one pattern to watch.
- **What does a win look like?** Possibly a *quiet* one: something that operates in a niche or at small scale and solves a real problem; incremental collaborations with established brands.
- **Every company has a missing factor:** sustainability (life-cycle assessments are likely to be gate-to-gate rather than cradle-to-gate) or price.
- **Open questions:** why does no one want to wear wool? Is the protein trend engineered? Should the focus be novel bio-based materials? And is it always down to regulation, so should we lobby government? Local production and incentives.
