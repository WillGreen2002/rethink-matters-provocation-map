# Assessing past biobased-materials start-ups

*RM007 (2026-07-11)* · notes RM007.4

## Provocation

> Assessing past biobased materials start-ups, especially the downturn around 2023 to 2024. The main examples are textile start-ups. What tensions made expectations hard to meet? Timing, investor expectations, product–market fit, sustainability claims and regulation or policy hurdles. What's the latest on biobased material companies?

## Post-meeting synthesis (anonymised digest)

- **Why look back?** Venture-capital funding is a challenge for the “second wave” of companies, so how do you build a biomaterial company? Tensions in earlier ventures: timing, investor expectations, product–market fit, sustainability claims, regulation and policy hurdles.
- **Range of examples discussed:** stretchable and elastic bio-based materials, mycelium-based materials, bacterial-cellulose products and peptide adhesives. One pattern to watch: pressure to deliver can lead companies to change direction.
- **What does a win look like?** Possibly a *quiet* one: something that operates in a niche or at small scale and solves a real problem; incremental collaborations with established brands.
- **A common missing factor:** sustainability (life-cycle assessments may be gate-to-gate rather than cradle-to-gate) or price.
- **Open questions:** why does no one want to wear wool? What is behind the protein trend? Should the focus be novel bio-based materials? And is it always down to regulation, so should we lobby government? Local production and incentives.
