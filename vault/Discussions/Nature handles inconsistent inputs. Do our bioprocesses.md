# Nature handles inconsistent inputs. Do our bioprocesses?

*RM006 (2026-06-20)* · notes RM006.9

## Provocation

> Nature handles inconsistent inputs remarkably well. Every living system evolved to tolerate variance or scavenge opportunistically. But when we industrialise biology and try to replicate those same processes in a bioreactor, we tend to engineer out the adaptability entirely. Bioprocesses are typically built around defined input, defined output. Waste streams are challenging to work with because of that: variable, unpredictable, and inconsistent by nature. So if waste feedstocks are central to achieving genuine circularity in bio-based materials manufacturing, will the processes be so complex that we're engineering ourselves out of ever reaching scale. And if that's not inevitable, what would an adaptive manufacturing system that actually thrives on inconsistency look like?
