# Should biomaterials research be limited to the microscale?

*RM006 (2026-06-20)* · notes RM006.11

## Provocation

> Should biomaterials research be limited to the microscale? Do bulk biomaterials as a replacement/ drop in substitution fail on energy economy and unnecessary functionality?
> 
> Microscale biomaterial (surgical scaffolds, metal ion patternation via 3D multicellular structures, high contrast medical nanoparticles for tissue delivery, etc) can offer a clear step change in functionality and or manufacturability. Does this still hold when we have advanced models for organism genetic manipulation (+5-10 years) and we can design hosts to take on higher order programming.

## Post-meeting synthesis (anonymised digest)

- **Overall stance:** the group mostly pushed back on the micro-scale framing — macroscale biomaterials already dominate (agriculture has shaped living materials since prehistory; wood is the most abundant biomaterial; bulk biomaterials do not have to be genetically modified to count as biotechnology) — but agreed that micro-scale precision is essential for medical applications.
- **Growing bulk materials in bioreactors:** on *energy economy*, simple monomers are cheap and a fair drop-in while complex materials (for example with integrated sensing) get inefficient quickly; it was suggested that a bioprocess can rival harsh chemical synthesis on energy. On *quality versus speed*, can fast bioreactor cellulose ever match hardwood? Ideas: design directly for hardwood composition and lignin ratios; “overclock” trees by simulating seasons in controlled warehouses.
- **Where micro-scale wins:** precision patterning is irreplaceable for in-vitro organs, prosthetics, bone grafts (3D-printed scaffolds, hydrogels with stem cells or growth factors), cartilage and collagen composites; cultivated meat also needs scaffolds. Lipid nanoparticles in vaccines can programme release kinetics (for example a delayed booster).
- **Higher-order programming can also come from physics:** self-organisation and excitable-media dynamics (for example spiral calcium waves in heart cells) give large-scale order from geometry alone — a counterpoint to the premise that advanced genetic manipulation is the route.
- **Circularity tangent:** modified wood and cross-laminated-timber buildings; a pitch for brick-style deconstructable buildings so that treated wood can be reused rather than landfilled — framed as a policy problem with our linear system.
