# Is there a rationale for ocean habitation?

*RM010 (2026-09-12)* · notes RM010.2

## Provocation

> Is there a rationale for ocean habitation? What material advancements now would become necessary to sustain human life underwater? What prospecting now is missing to model ocean ecosystem and geosystems? Are there any possibilities of nanoscale or microscale materials that need to scale up for this to exist? And what do we do about halotolerance?

## Post-meeting synthesis (anonymised digest)

- **Definition:** no consensus on what “ocean habitation” means; the agreed definition was *underwater*, not merely floating (stilt and piloti houses excluded).
- **Science case vs habitation case:** the science case is strong (a vast unmapped seabed; bioprospecting and sequencing efforts, for example plastic-adapted microbes from pressurised seafloor samples). The habitation case is weaker: hard to justify beyond research and niche industrial uses (high-pressure processes, deep-sea mining, bioprospecting). The ocean is easier to return from than space, and resilience is higher (desalination, reverse osmosis, semi-enclosed water systems).
- **Challenges:** pressure, darkness, circadian disruption and loneliness; viable depth limited by light; the longest recorded submarine stay (about 200 days) had a documented psychological toll.
- **Structural concepts debated:** static anchored modules near geothermal vents (energy, manufacturing); free-floating, current-propelled structures (currents as an energy source); modular bubble clusters (if one fails others survive; can grow stepwise); a 15 km vertical “Ocean Spiral” concept for CO₂ storage and energy generation.
- **Materials:** titanium resists pressure but suffers biofouling (microstructured coatings, or deliberate biofouling, as possible answers); hydrogels’ viscoelastic and hydrating properties for structural or lifestyle roles; biomineralisation under ocean acidification as a design constraint; rigid–elastic hybrids to absorb current stress; bioluminescence or chemiluminescence for light; photo-initiated resins for underwater 3D printing; metamaterials that filter wavelengths. Deep-water coral at 300–400 m is a biological precedent. Modules likely need to be built above sea level and submerged.
