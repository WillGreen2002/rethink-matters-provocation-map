# Should other macromolecules be more like DNA? (“DNA is fancy PEG”)

*RM007 (2026-07-11)* · notes RM007.5

## Provocation

> Should we be researching how to make biological macromolecules more like DNA? In DNA-based technologies we can cheaply synthesise, assemble, lab-scale scale-up, sequence, and therefore assemble in the most programmable matter. Where are the caveats for glycans, proteins, lipids, and even RNA? Or RNA modifications like locked nucleic acids? Ignoring some of the classic caveats against DNA origami, so considering beyond DNA as the sole material, what about synthesising composite polymers that can retain the valuable properties of all these types of biomacromolecules?
> 
> DNA is basically fancy PEG. That is; biomaterials can be regarded as "similar" or general along many property axis and only require special consideration within specific limited dimensions. PEG cant be read by DNApol but DNA can be a floppy crowding agent.
