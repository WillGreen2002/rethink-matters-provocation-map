# Control, or let it grow?

Machines give control; living and soft matter give growth, hierarchy and emergence. The question running through these notes is how to combine them — and what we are optimising for.

1. [[Patterning to organs]] — We can pattern cells and build organoids, but not full-size vascularised organs: the spatial coordination of growth outruns one-dimensional control.
2. [[Signalling & crosstalk]] — Patterns are built from signals; holding them means insulating against crosstalk — and asking how robust is too robust.
3. [[Printing meets growth]] — A hybrid logic: machines impose boundary conditions while the material organises itself, shifting design from final form to initial conditions.
4. [[Fabrication readiness]] — Which printing, microfluidic or volumetric methods are actually ready, and for what?
5. [[Material 'intelligence']] — At what point does aligned, hierarchical, self-repairing structure begin to look like a primitive living material?
6. [[Portable design principles]] — If hierarchy, self-assembly and damage tolerance are portable principles, we should design with them rather than copy their parts.

**So what for the central question:** How much control is wise? The answer decides whether we design outcomes or design conditions — and how much unpredictability we are prepared to live with.
