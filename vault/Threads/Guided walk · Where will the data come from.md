# Where will the data come from?

Almost every theme hits the same wall: we cannot predict or design what we cannot measure. This trail follows the data bottleneck from properties we barely understand to the sensors that could supply the missing readout.

1. [[Emergent properties]]: Emergent properties depend on patterning, alignment and signalling, so composition alone is a poor predictor, and we may not understand the logic behind the patterns.
2. [[Public datasets]]: Useful public datasets need agreement on what to measure and which inputs and outcomes to record.
3. [[AI meets biological constraints]]: Models trained the mainstream way meet their limits where emergent behaviour is the target: a single percentage point of error can separate a working drug from a failed one.
4. [[World models & inverse design]]: World models and inverse design might predict how multicellular materials develop, but they need experimental feedback to learn from.
5. [[Variant-library screening]]: We can build a million variants yet read out only a few hundred samples a day: a millionth of the fitness landscape.
6. [[Biosensor design]]: Biosensors that turn molecular binding into a signal could supply the readout, if allostery, evolutionary escape and quantitative detection can be solved.

**So what for the central question:** A world we cannot measure is a world we cannot responsibly engineer: data infrastructure is a precondition for humility.
