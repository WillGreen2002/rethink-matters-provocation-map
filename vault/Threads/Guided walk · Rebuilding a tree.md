# Rebuilding a tree

Wood is already self-assembling, hierarchical, load-bearing, cheap and carbon-storing. Starting from that benchmark, these notes ask what a bio-based material should do instead of imitating the incumbent.

1. [[Engineering a tree]]: What would a tree look like if engineered from day one, and what are we missing to predict and design it?
2. [[Cellulose, wood & concrete]]: Bacterial cellulose as a wood substitute is the trap; the better question is what it can do that wood never could. The same logic applies to biomineral versus concrete.
3. [[Hydrated materials]]: Bacterial cellulose is 98–99% water: hydration is the barrier to dropping it into the dry world.
4. [[Replace or reinvent]]: Biobased materials may win by offering functions incumbents cannot: responsiveness, repair, biodegradation, sensing, local production.
5. [[Micro vs bulk]]: Bulk biomaterials already dominate (agriculture, wood), while micro-scale precision is essential for medicine.
6. [[Fabrication readiness]]: Cultured wood, anisotropy engineering and volumetric printing: which routes are ready?

**So what for the central question:** Learning from nature may mean asking what biology makes possible, not how closely we can copy it.
