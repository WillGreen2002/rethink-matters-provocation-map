# Access, safety, and who is responsible

Wider access to biotechnology, devices and diagnostics widens exposure to risk. These notes keep asking who is accountable when things go wrong.

1. [[Care & convivial tech]]: Convivial technology widens access but needs a safety row; some risks can only be handled through shared facilities and regulation.
2. [[Designed-peptide risks]]: Designed peptides bring aggregation and toxicity risks that current models predict poorly.
3. [[Infection-sensing dressings]]: At-home diagnostics trade ease of use against misreading or delayed care.
4. [[Human–machine integration]]: When an implant depends on a company's software, who is accountable if the company disappears?
5. [[Duties to organisms]]: Responsibility does not stop with humans: what do we owe the organisms we use?
6. [[Salt tolerance & ecosystems]]: …or the ecosystems we intervene in: the consequences of marine interventions need modelling before we act.

**So what for the central question:** Safety and access are not opposites if accountability is designed in from the start.
