# “Bio” is not a performance claim

‘Bio’ describes origin, or contact with tissue — not safety, biodegradability or sustainability. This trail follows that ambiguity from definitions through ventures and hype to long-term evidence.

1. [[Biobased vs biomaterial]] — ‘Biomaterial’ can mean use in the body, interaction with living systems, or biological origin.
2. [['Bio' ≠ good]] — Biological origin alone does not establish environmental or ethical value.
3. [[Lessons from ventures]] — Investor expectations and sustainability claims are among the tensions the group examined in the last wave of biobased-textile ventures.
4. [[Hype vs evidence]] — ‘Breakthrough versus hype’ is a false binary; evidence needs plain-language reporting and honest uncertainty.
5. [[Chronic bioelectrodes]] — Even in medicine, a promising route can be overhyped when long-term stability data is missing.

**So what for the central question:** Claims travel faster than evidence. Being exact about what ‘bio’ does and does not promise is a form of humility — and of governance.
