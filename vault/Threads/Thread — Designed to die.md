# Designed to die

Biology always wins — materials degrade, living ones need care, and we have little agency over either. This trail follows end of life from definitions to disposal.

1. [[Living vs formerly living]] — A cotton shirt was once living; a microbial textile with growth halted may still count as living — definitions have consequences.
2. [[Hydrated materials]] — Water invites life, and so degradation. We have little control over the moment a material stops being functional.
3. [[Survive use, degrade after]] — Materials must survive use and climate extremes yet degrade after disposal: reliable triggers, or dedicated collection systems?
4. [[Discovery vs reuse]] — Every new material creates a future end-of-life problem: do we need more materials, or better reuse of the ones we have?
5. [[Chronic bioelectrodes]] — In the body, degradation timelines are a key worry for conductive-polymer electrodes.
6. [[When to let materials die]] — For living materials, withholding care is the end-of-life trigger: which living functions justify ongoing moisture, nutrients and care?

**So what for the central question:** Designing a good end of life is part of designing the material — and, for living materials, an ethical choice.
